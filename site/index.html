Do

  • Keep clear space equal to the chevron height.
  • Use the dark logo on light backgrounds and the white logo on dark or photographic backgrounds.
  • Use approved logo files (vector formats like SVG or EPS when possible) rather than screenshots or low-res copies.
  • Maintain clear space and padding around the logo as specified in these guidelines.
  • Scale proportionally, keeping the aspect ratio locked.
  • Ensure sufficient contrast between the logo and its background.
  • Place on clean, uncluttered backgrounds where possible.
  • Follow minimum size requirements (25 mm wide in print / 50 px wide digitally) so details remain legible.

Don’t

  • Stretch, squash, or distort the logo.
  • Change the logo’s colors outside of approved variations.
  • Rotate or skew the logo.
  • Add effects like shadows, outlines, gradients, or glows.
  • Place the logo on busy or low-contrast backgrounds.
  • Recreate the logo using a different font or redraw it.
  • Crop or overlap the logo with other elements.
  • Use outdated or unapproved versions of the logo (yellow).
  • Change the spacing between the word and the chevron.

      This editorial style guide outlines the preferred writing style and formatting rules for all content produced by Smart Communications. By following these guidelines, we ensure consistency and coherence across all written materials.

      Point of view

      Wherever possible, we use a second-person point of view to establish a connection with our audience and foster engagement.

      Do · second person
      “Engage your customers with tailored and personalized communications by following these five steps.”

      The writer speaks directly to the audience, using “you” as the subject. This creates engagement and empowers the reader to act.

      Avoid · third person
      “Organizations can engage their customers with tailored and personalized communications by following these five steps.”

      Referring to “organizations” as the subject creates distance between the writer and the audience and can feel impersonal.

      Active vs. passive voice

      In the active voice, the subject of the sentence performs the action. It makes writing more direct, engaging and easier to understand.

      Do · active voice
      “You can customize and deliver notifications to your customers in their preferred channels.”

      The subject is “you,” which is more personal, and it emphasizes what the reader can do (“customize” and “deliver”).

      Avoid · passive voice
      “Notifications can be customized and delivered to customers in their preferred channels.”

      Still informative, but the subject is “notifications,” so it feels impersonal and vague.

      We use American English as the standard language for our content.

      Language

      • Use American spelling, such as color, center and organize.
      • Spell out acronyms such as “CCM” on first reference.
      • No need to spell out commonly known industry acronyms such as HIPAA, GDPR, SOC1 and P&C.

      When writing specifically for a British or Australian audience, use British English and standard British rules for dates, times and numerals.

      Dates + times

      • Month – day – year, with a comma after the day and year (e.g. June 1, 2023).
      • British English: day – month – year with no commas (e.g. 1 June 2023).
      • Use ordinal numbers for the day where applicable (e.g. June 1st, 2023).
      • Lowercase am or pm (not a.m. and p.m.).
      • Leave a space before am or pm, and use a colon between hours and minutes (e.g. 9:00 am).
      • Always include minutes (9:00 am, not 9 am).

      Number values

      • Spell out numbers under ten (“two”, not “2”).
      • Use numerals for 10 or greater (“10”, not “ten”).
      • Use numerals for measurements (“5 pounds”).
      • Use the % symbol (20%, not “20 percent”).
      • Use commas in thousands and higher (“1,000”).
      • Keep number style consistent throughout a piece.

      These rules are for long-form content such as blogs and white papers. They don’t apply to social posts, digital ads or infographics where space is limited.

      Punctuation

      Oxford comma

      For clarity, we use the Oxford comma — the comma before the conjunction in a list of three or more items.

      Bulleted lists

      Full sentences: use punctuation. Short phrases: no punctuation needed.

      Em dash (—)

      Sets off a phrase or clause, signals a break in thought, or adds emphasis.

      Title case and sentence case

      We use title case for main headlines and sentence case for subheadlines and body copy to maintain a clear, consistent hierarchy across brand communications.

      Title case — Main headlines

      Capitalize major words, including nouns, pronouns, verbs, adjectives, and adverbs. Keep short words such as to, and, or, at, by, of, and for lowercase unless they appear at the beginning or end of a headline.

      Sentence case — Subheadlines and body copy

      Capitalize only the first word and proper nouns, including official brand and product names. Use standard sentence punctuation for body copy.

      Examples

      Main headline (title case): How to Create a Winning Marketing Strategy
      Subheadline (sentence case): A step-by-step guide to success
      Body copy (sentence case): Discover how to build a marketing strategy that drives meaningful results.

      Avoid ending headlines and subheadlines with punctuation. Keep headlines concise, ideally 10 words or fewer.

            Filming customer stories suggestions

            Film your customers seated and in conversation, so the story feels natural and unscripted. Choose a simple background that keeps the focus on the person, such as a dark textured backdrop, a green screen, or a natural, neutral setting with a softly blurred background. Frame the speaker slightly off-center and direct their eyeline just off camera toward the interviewer. Include a second camera shot with a tighter close-up for variety, to help with editing and adding emotional emphasis. Light the subject’s face softly and evenly, separate the person from the background, and keep skin tones warm and natural. Ask each person to repeat the question back before they answer it, so their response makes sense on its own when the interviewer’s voice is removed in the edit. Let people be themselves, and capture genuine reactions, such as a pause, a smile, or a moment of emphasis, instead of polished delivery.

            Please note, this section is only applicable when filming people.

            Video specs and format

            Export final videos as MP4 files. Most videos, including customer stories, product videos, and website content, are exported at 1920×1080px (16:9, landscape). For organic social posts, export a specialty size at 1080×1350px (4:5, portrait), also as an MP4.
